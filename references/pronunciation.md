# Polish Pronunciation for Arabic Speakers

## The Golden Rule
Polish is phonetic — every letter has one fixed sound. Unlike English (or even Arabic
with its context-dependent sounds), Polish reads exactly as written. This is a major
advantage for Arabic speakers who are already used to precise phoneme-to-letter mapping.

---

## Arabic Speaker Advantages

| Polish Sound | IPA | Arabic Equivalent | Notes |
|-------------|-----|-------------------|-------|
| **r** | /r/ | ر | Rolled r — identical to Arabic ر |
| **ch / h** | /x/ | خ | Identical to Arabic خ (kha) |
| **sz** | /ʃ/ | ش | Identical to Arabic ش (sha) |
| **cz** | /tʃ/ | تش | Like تش in تشيلي |
| **rz / ż** | /ʒ/ | ژ | Like French "j" — no Arabic exact match, but close to soft ج |
| **ł** | /w/ | و | Like English W, or Arabic و |
| **w** | /v/ | ف | Like English V — Arabic speakers often say ف |
| **j** | /j/ | ي | Identical to Arabic ي |
| **ń** | /ɲ/ | ني | Like نيا in a single sound |

## Stress Rule
Polish stress is almost always on the **penultimate syllable** (second from last).
- **war**-sza-wa → WarSZAwa ← stress on SZA
- **ka**-wa → KAwa ← stress on KA
- **dzie**-ku-je → dzięKUję ← stress on KU

**Exception:** Some loan words and verbs in past tense 1st/2nd person plural stress the antepenultimate (third from last): **by**-li-śmy.

## Polish Alphabet — Quick Reference

```
A  Ą  B  C  Ć  D  E  Ę  F  G  H  I  J  K  L  Ł
M  N  Ń  O  Ó  P  R  S  Ś  T  U  W  Y  Z  Ź  Ż
```

Letters NOT in Polish: Q, V, X (appear only in foreign words)
